Staying organised and focused is a common and growing challenge in our digital age, with the relentless torrent of novelty and associated complexity.

A common difficulty is having information spread across many separate websites, applications and systems.

Take, for example, planning a holiday trip.

You might have any number of the following...

- Some wish list apartment or room stays in **AirBnB**.
- Some hotel or hostel bookings in **Booking.com**.
- Tour bookings and flight plans in **GMail**.
- Sight seeing recommendations from a friend in your **Notes App**.

You can see the complexity of managing all this scattered information.

In the past, various solutions tried to address this. Notebook apps, reminder apps, todo apps. Now there are a myriad of AI solutions claiming to solve it, with varying results. The problem with many of these "solutions" is that they are trying to force together information which sits in different systems. This creates various frictions and makes the solution a headache in itself.

An alternative is: leave the data where it is and just enforce a common organisational pattern. Instead of trying to force information together, focus on making the information as findable as possible. To this end, I've come up with what I term a "universal folder structure" for organising my life. I use this common structure to organise all the information across all systems I use.

For example, the travel part of my folder structure looks like this:

- 📁 Travel
  - 📁 [Departure Date] (for example, "2029-01-01")
    - 📁 [Country] (for example, "Singapore, US, Canada, Italy")
      - 📁 [City] (for example, "Singapore, Arizona, Toronto, Rome")
        - 📁 Stays
        - 📁 Flights
        - 📁 Sights

Suppose I'm planning travel to the US and Singapore. I might develop the following structure:

- 📁 Travel
  - 📁 2029-01-01
    - 📁 US
      - 📁 LA
        - 📁 Flights
          - 📄 SYD-LAX
        - 📁 Stays
          - 📄 2029-01-03-venice-beach-hostel
      - 📁 Arizona
        - 📁 Stays
          - 📄 2029-01-07-springhill-suites-marriott
        - 📁 Flights
          - 📄 LAX-AZ
        - 📁 Sights
          - 📄 Grand Canyon
    - 📁 Singapore
      - 📁 Dempsey Hills
        - 📁 Stays
          - 📄 airbnb-2029-01-14
        - 📁 Sights
          - 📄 open-farm-community

I simply replicate the applicable parts of the structure in each system I have an account in.

For example, in my **AirBnB account**, I use their "wishlists", with slash-delimited ("/") names, to create a nested structure.

- 🩶 Singapore / Dempsey Hills
- 🩶 US / AZ
- 🩶 Canada / Toronto

Similarly, for my **Booking.com account**, I use **browser bookmarks** with the same structure:

- 📁 Travel
  - 📁 2029-01-01
    - 📁 US
      - 📁 Arizona
        - 📁 Stays
          - 🌏 Venice Beach Hostel | My Bookings | Booking.com
      - 📁 LA
        - 📁 Stays
          - 🌏 Springhill Suites Marriott | My Bookings | Booking.com

And, for my flight and tour bookings, I use **Labels in GMail** with the same structure:

- 🏷️ Travel
  - 🏷️ 2029-01-01
    - 🏷️ US
      - 🏷️ Arizona
        - 🏷️ Flights
          - 📧 LAX-AZ
        - 🏷️ Sights
      - 🏷️ LA
        - 🏷️ Flights
          - 📧 SYD-LAX
        - 🏷️ Sights
          - 📧 Tour of Grand Canyon | Booking Confirmation
    - 🏷️ Singapore
      - 🏷️ Dempsey Hills
          - 📧 Core Collective Yoga | Booking Confirmation

The same kind of method can be applied to any kind of personal information.

- Health
- Job applications
- Study
- Finances
- Investments
- Purchases
- etc...

This organisational method works well for me. When I need to access a particular piece of information (such as checking the address of an AirBnB booking or checking in to a flight) I simply navigate to the appropriate part of the folder structure in the appropriate system (AirBnB or GMail).

Will this ever be as nice as having everything in one system with zero friction or overhead? No. But as far I know, no such system exists.

For the time being this is good enough. I won't let the perfect be the enemy of the good.
